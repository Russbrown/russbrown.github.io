What I have always loved about people who work on the web is the compulsion to share what they have learned. I would not have a job right now if it wasn’t for the selfless efforts of hundreds of tutorial writers, bloggers, comment-repliers and demo-ers. I’ve been reading a book lately, one of those books you know is great because everyone cites it but you never get around to reading because you’re reading a Hornblower novel.

Thinking Fast & Slow by Daniel Kahneman is one of the the most interesting books I have ever read. Hands down. It is literally rammed with information. Seriously. Three pages of this book contains enough of your RDA of science for the whole week. Studies, experiments, puzzles, tricks and stories are all shoehorned into these pages like a Sasquatch trying to get into ballet shoes.

Here is a presentation I’ve given a couple of times about the things I learned from the first half of the book and how they relate to visual design on the web. I hope its useful and encourages others to dust off that copy they got for Christmas or to finally pluck it from their Amazon wishlist and add it to their cart.
